# Agent handoff and synthesis contract

All six native Codex agents use the shared version 1 schema at `assets/handoff.schema.json`. First validate saved-file persistence and handoff shape with `python scripts/validate_handoff_artifact.py handoff.json`; then validate collection/review lifecycle with `python scripts/validate_handoffs.py handoff.json [--synthesis contract.json]`. Artifact generation, successful persistence, schema validation, and lifecycle acceptance are separate states. A missing, empty, malformed, or nonconforming file is never a successfully persisted artifact. `COMPLETE`, `PARTIAL`, `FAILED`, and `BLOCKED` describe task execution only; none asserts evidence success. Claim evidence retains the existing `CORROBORATED`, `SINGLE_SOURCE`, `CONFLICTED`, `UNVERIFIED` statuses; `UNAVAILABLE` describes provider/observation access.

Each claim references observation IDs. Observations capture source, exact tool, upstream provenance when available, asset and venue identity, metric/value/unit, observation and retrieval UTC times, and methodology/coverage when available. Unknown provenance or methodology remains null or omitted; do not fabricate it. Tool call summaries record successes, failures, unavailable providers, and tools not called. A `CORROBORATED` ledger claim requires distinct documented upstream origins and documented measurement methods. For claims typed `TOKEN_CONTRACT_IDENTITY` or `ON_CHAIN_IDENTITY`, it also requires explicit compatible chain IDs. Chain qualification is not universally required for market prices or exchange instruments. Provider count or a matching contract string alone cannot establish independence; retain `SINGLE_SOURCE` when independence is unknown.

## Collection and review lifecycle

The primary agent alone owns scope, dispatch, specialist collection, reconciliation, synthesis, and final conclusions. Dispatch only relevant specialists, with at most three concurrent specialists. Agents do not delegate recursively. Close specialist collection only after every selected specialist has returned a structurally valid handoff; statuses may be PARTIAL, FAILED, or BLOCKED and must not be rewritten as COMPLETE. Reconcile those reports before freezing a synthesis revision. Only then may the adversarial reviewer and evidence verifier run.

Reviewer handoffs must include `review.synthesis_revision`, the frozen synthesis SHA-256, and the exact `claim_ids` reviewed. Freeze the input as a canonical JSON value: serialize with sorted keys, compact separators, UTF-8, then compute lowercase SHA-256. Store both snapshot and digest with revision ID. Validate digest before accepting review output. This convention detects accidental mismatch; it does not make storage tamperproof, and Codex does not natively enforce immutability. Validators check declared structure and bindings, not that review reasoning is correct or that observations are true.

The synthesis contract accepted by the validator has `synthesis_owner`, `specialist_phase` (`state`: `OPEN` or `CLOSED`, `expected_agent_ids`), `reconciliation.completed`, `revision` (`id`, `snapshot`, `sha256`), and `reviews`. Select at most three specialists. Review entries must not be present until collection is closed and reconciliation is complete. All expected specialists must have submitted a handoff before closure; incomplete execution remains explicit and can prevent a defensible research conclusion. Reviewer handoffs supplied alongside the synthesis contract are checked against the revision ID, digest, and claim IDs found in the frozen snapshot.
